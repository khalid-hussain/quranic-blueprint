
# About the Author {.unnumbered}

Shaykh Muhammad Owais ibn Muḥammad Anīs ibn Muḥammad Idrīs ibn Ḥāfiẓ Sayyid ʿAbd
al-ʿAlī al-Nagrāmī al-Nadwī was born on 4 January 1914 in Nagrām, Lucknow, into
an illustrious family of scholars whose educational, religious, political, and
reformative services left a deep imprint upon Awadh (modern-day Uttar Pradesh in
India) and beyond.

His birth coincided with a moment of symbolic transition in Indian Islamic
scholarship. He was born in the very year that ʿAllāmah Shiblī Nuʿmānī (d.
1914), a radiant sun of knowledge, reform, and intellectual revival, passed
away. This coincidence recalls a familiar pattern in Islamic scholarly history:
just as Imam al-Shāfiʿī was born in the year Imam Abū Ḥanīfah passed away, so
too, with the setting of one great sun of learning, another quietly rose upon
the land of Nagrām.

Shaykh Abū al-ḤasanʿAlī Nadwī \hon{\M_HIM}{\BODY} wrote, "In the thirteenth
century Hijrī, through Allāh's special Mercy, a new wave of guidance and reform
appeared in this region. Among the many scholarly families of Awadh, there were
two noble lineages that remained firm upon Tawḥīd, adherence to the Sunnah, and
the revival of pure Islamic teachings. One was the family of Ḥaḍrat Sayyid Aḥmad
Shahīd \hon{\M_HIM}{\BODY}, whose spiritual center was Takiyah Kalān, Rae
Bareilly (the lineage of ʿAlī Miyan himself). The other was the family of
Ḥaḍrat Mawlānā Ḥāfiẓ ʿAbd al-ʿAlī Nagrāmī \hon{\M_HIM}{\BODY}, based in Nagrām,
Lucknow (known as the ʿUlamāʾ of Nagrām). Together, these two families played a
foundational role in shaping the religious and spiritual legacy of the region."
(Purāne Charāgh)

The Shaykh's great-grandfather, Shaykh ʿAbd al-ʿAlī ibn Pīr ʿAlī ibn Ghulām Imam
al-Hindī al-Nagrāmī, was born in the year 1131\SC{h} (1719\SC{ce}). He authored and
compiled works, and studied the religious sciences under several scholars such
as his maternal uncle Sayyid Anwar ʿAlī al-Murādābādī, Shaykh Aḥmad al-Bakrī
al-Nagrāmī, Shaykh ʿAbd al-Ḥakīm ibn ʿAbd al-Rabb al-Kahawnawī, and many other
scholars. He studied the spiritual sciences with al-Qāḍī ʿAbd al-Karīm
al-Nagrāmī. He also received authorization (_ijāzah_) from Shaykh Bināʾ ʿAṭāʾ
al-Salūnī and Khwāja Aḥmad ibn Yāsīn al-Naṣīrābādī.

Shaykh ʿAbd al-ʿAlī was a man of piety, righteousness, chastity, and trust in
Allāh. The people around him benefited from his good character even before they
benefited from his knowledge. Allāh guided many people to accept Islam at his
hands. He authored numerous works, among the most famous of which is _Tafsīr Āyāt
al-Aḥkām_.

From an academic perspective, his _Tafsīr Āyāt al-Aḥkām_ holds a distinguished
status. Its most notable feature is how the Quranic _āyāt_ are arranged
according to jurisprudential order. Previously, works on Aḥkām al-Qurʾān
followed the _sūrah_-based sequence, but in this book, legal chapters were first
established, followed by sections under which relevant _āyāt_ were collected.
Then came the translation, followed by explanatory marginal notes. The legal
positions of the four Imams were also mentioned. Among the books written in India
on Aḥkām al-Qurʾān, this work holds significant importance. Accordingly, the
renowned Indian historian and scholar Mawlānā Sayyid ʿAbd al-Ḥayy al-Ḥasanī
\hon{\M_HIM}{\BODY} mentioned it in _al-Thaqāfah al-Islāmīyah fī al-Hind_. When
Nawab Ṣiddīq Ḥasan Khān \hon{\M_HIM}{\BODY} received this book, he wrote a
letter in Arabic to Ḥāfiẓ ʿAbd al-ʿAlī \hon{\M_HIM}{\BODY}, stating, after
praising his knowledge and scholarship, that although he himself had written a
book on Aḥkām al-Qurʾān, Ḥāfiẓ Ṣāḥib's work surpassed his own in scholarly
merit. This entire letter was published in the _Maʿārif_ magazine.

Shaykh ʿAbd al-ʿAlī passed away on the night of Wednesday, the 6th of Shawwāl,
in the year 1296\SCP{h} (1782\SC{ce}). His son, Idrīs ibn ʿAbd al-ʿAlī,
performed his funeral rites. May Allāh have mercy on him.

The Shaykh's grandfather, Shaykh Muḥammad Idrīs al-Nagrāmī, was born on Monday,
11th of Shawwāl 1275\SC{h} (1859\SC{ce}), in Nagrām, Lucknow. He was highly
distinguished in knowledge, virtue, righteousness, and piety. Besides studying
under his father, he acquired knowledge from the esteemed Shaykh ʿAbd al-Ḥayy
Farangī Maḥallī, Mawlānā ʿAbd al-Raḥmān Pānīpatī, and Shaykh ʿAbd al-Ḥaqq, the
author of _Tafsīr Ḥaqqānī_. In the spiritual sciences, he benefited from the
spiritual guide of the age, Mawlānā Faḍl al-Raḥmān of Ganj Muradabad and his
maternal uncle, the late ʿAbd al-Salām ibn Abī al-Qāsim al-Husaynī al-Wāsiṭī.
Thus he attained a lofty rank in both the outward and inward sciences.

The Shaykh authored many works, and he's most renowned for his seminal
biographical work _ʿUlamāʾ-i Ḥāl_, which resurrects the legacies of 423
pioneering Islamic scholars from the Indian subcontinent of the 14th century
till his time. This work was first published in 1315\SC{h} (1897\SC{ce}). He
established and ran a Dār al-Iftāʾ in Nagrām decades before the very
establishment of Nadwat al-ʿUlamāʾ. The Shaykh authored more than 60 other
works, including glosses on many books, nearly all of which are lost today.

There were thousands of books in the family library, many of which were copied
meticulously by hand by Shaykh Idrīs. He spent 30 years doing this and many a
time had nothing to eat. His nights were like his days, where he was in the
constant company of books. Shaykh Idrīs established Maṭbaʿ-i Nafīsī Publication
in Nagrām to spread authentic teachings of Islam and rectify the wrong practices
that had crept into the lives of the people.

Shaykh Muḥammad ʿAlī Mungeri (the founder of Nadwat al-ʿUlamāʾ) personally
requested Shaykh Idrīs Nagrāmī to have his family become a part of the newly
born Dārul ʿUlūm Nadwat al-ʿUlamāʾ in Lucknow. Heeding this call, Shaykh Idrīs
enrolled his sons, Anīs and Nafīs Nagrāmī, at the institution. Shaykh Idrīs
passed away on the 10th of Ramaḍān in the year 1330\SC{h} (23 August
1912\SC{ce}).

The late Mawlānā's father, Maulānā Muḥammad Anīs Nagrāmī, was likewise a
well-known scholar who regularly undertook preaching and reformative tours
throughout the towns and villages of Awadh.

Shaykh Owais's father, Shaykh Muḥammad Anīs Nagrāmī, alongside figures such as
Shaykh Muḥammad Nafīs Nagrāmī, Shaykh Muḥammad Idrīs Nagrāmī, Shaykh ʿAbd
al-Raḥmān Nagrāmī, and Shaykh Ḥusayn Aḥmad Madanī, actively participated in
India's freedom struggle while simultaneously leading sustained efforts against
shirk, heretical beliefs, and corrupt social practices throughout Awadh. They
visited many remote areas in harsh conditions on foot and using bullock carts.
This kind of outreach is not something many of us can even imagine doing today.

In this way, the late Mawlānā inherited both the outward and inward sciences,
and the proclamations of "Qāla Allāh wa Qāla al-Rasūl" reached his ears even
before he came of age. As he advanced in life, he greatly enriched this
priceless inheritance and spread the name and work of his forefathers far and
wide.

Shaykh Owais began his early education in Nagrām. A praiseworthy trait observed
within the family of Shaykh Owais was that a son would first study the
foundational sciences and manners (_ādāb_) at the feet of his father before
proceeding to other teachers. Thus, we see Shaykh Owais learning from Shaykh
Anīs, Shaykh Anīs learning from Shaykh Idrīs, and likewise Shaykh Idrīs learning
from Shaykh Ḥāfiẓ Sayyid ʿAbd al-ʿAlī. May Allāh have Mercy upon them all.

He later enrolled at Dār al-ʿUlūm Nadwat al-ʿUlamāʾ in 1927, where he studied
until 1932 under some of the most distinguished scholars of the age. After
completing his formal studies, he pursued advanced training in the Six Books of
Ḥadīth, with special emphasis on _Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī_ and _Jāmiʿ al-Tirmidhī_,
under Shaykh Ḥusayn Aḥmad Madanī \hon{\M_HIM}{\BODY}, then principal of Dār
al-ʿUlūm Deoband.

The intellectual refinement and cultivation he acquired were also deeply shaped
by the environment of Nadwah itself. Furthermore, he had the opportunity to
benefit from the incomparable Sayyid al-Ṭāʾifah ʿAllāmah Sayyid Sulaymān Nadwī
\hon{\M_HIM}{\BODY}, whose discerning gaze could transform raw metal into pure
gold. Shaykh Sayyid Sulaymān immediately recognized his intelligence and
potential, took him under his special supervision, and brought him to Dār
al-Muṣannifīn.

In 1939, Shaykh Owais proceeded to Dār al-Muṣannifīn. During this period, he was
profoundly influenced by the works of Shaykh al-Islām Ibn Taymīyah
\hon{\M_HIM}{\BODY}, Imam Ibn al-Qayyim \hon{\M_HIM}{\BODY}, and Shāh Walī Allāh
al-Dihlawī \hon{\M_HIM}{\BODY}, an influence that would later become evident in
both his teaching and his writings. He assisted ʿAllāmah Sayyid Sulaymān Nadwī
\hon{\M_HIM}{\BODY} in completing _Sīrat al-Nabī_, the monumental work initiated
by ʿAllāmah Shiblī Nuʿmānī \hon{\M_HIM}{\BODY}, and contributed to the research
and preparation of several other scholarly projects. He remained at Dār
al-Muṣannifīn for approximately seven years, teaching and writing. His articles
published in _Maʿārif_ became a focal point of attention for scholars. From the
outset, his inclination was toward Quranic sciences, and most of his writings
revolved around the Quran and its teachings.

Upon returning to Lucknow, Shaykh Owais was appointed Shaykh al-Tafsīr at Dār
al-ʿUlūm Nadwat al-ʿUlamāʾ on the recommendation of ʿAllāmah Sayyid Sulaymān
Nadwī \hon{\M_HIM}{\BODY}, who was one of the leading scholars of his time, the
Qāḍī of Bhopal, and one of the founding members of Nadwat al-ʿUlamāʾ. He held
this position for nearly four decades, quietly shaping generations of students
through deep Quranic insight, intellectual discipline, and spiritual sobriety.
He was married to the daughter of Shaykh Muḥammad Aṣghar Pratāpgarhī
\hon{\M_HIM}{\BODY} and was blessed to perform Ḥajj in 1948, 1953, and 1964.

Despite being offered prestigious academic posts, including a teaching position
at the newly established Islamic University of Madīnah, as well as appointments
in institutions in Peshawar and Calcutta, he declined them all, believing that
India was in greater need of his services. Instead, he sent his son, Prof. Dr.
Yūnus Nagrāmī \hon{\M_HIM}{\BODY}, to Madīnah with the first batch of Indian
students. Dr. Yūnus would later gain international recognition and become the
first Indian adviser to the Muslim World League, receiving the Presidential
Award of India in 1992 for his services to the Arabic language and literature.

Shaykh Owais toured and preached about the importance of Tawḥīd, warned against
innovations, and advocated for social reforms throughout his life in places like
Lucknow, Sultanpur, Rai Bareilly, Barabanki, and far-off villages, and made
arrangements for spreading the teachings of Islam in hundreds of homes.

Shaykh Owais was a colleague and close friend of Shaykh Abū al-Ḥasan ʿAlī Nadwī
\hon{\M_HIM}{\BODY} for nearly 25 years. Shaykh ʿAlī writes about him that,
apart from Shaykh Owais's services to the Quran, among his most impactful
contributions to Daʿwah was a public Tafsīr class held every Saturday after
Maghrib in Lucknow. This gathering attracted scholars, social elites, government
officials, and even British officers. Through it, Shaykh Owais conveyed the
Quran's authentic message, warned against innovations, and addressed
intellectual doubts, sparking the emergence of multiple Quranic study circles
across the city.

Shaykh ʿAbd al-Mājid Daryābādī \hon{\M_HIM}{\BODY} wrote in his preface to
_Qurʾān kā Muṭālaʿah Kaise?_, "Shaykh Owais is not merely a teacher of Tafsīr at
Dār al-ʿUlūm Nadwat al-ʿUlamāʾ with many years of experience in teaching the
Quran; rather, in terms of taste and intuition, he is truly---in the real
sense---a scholar and student of Quranic studies. His reading in this field is
extensive, and his insight, by the grace of Allāh, is remarkably deep. In every
respect, he has devoted himself to the service of the Quran. That is the bounty
of Allāh; He grants it to whom He wills."

His writings, spanning Arabic, Urdu, and Persian, can be found in some of the
most prestigious libraries and universities around the world. Some of his
published works include:

- _Tafsīr al-Qayyim_ (Arabic)
- _Taʿlīm al-Qurʾān_ (Urdu)
- _Al-ʿAqīdah al-Sunnīyah fī Sharḥ al-ʿAqīdah al-Ḥasanah_ (Arabic)
- _Al-Khayr al-Kathīr fī Sharḥ al-Fawz al-Kabīr_ (Arabic)
- _Rehnumāʾī ʿIlm al-Tafsīr_ (Persian)
- _Fiqh al-Qurʾān_ (Arabic)
- _Uṣūl-i Ḥadīth_ (Urdu)
- _Qurʾān kā Muṭālaʿah Kaise?_ (Urdu)
- _Taṣawwuf Kyā Hai?_ (Urdu)

Shaykh Owais compiled the scattered commentary on Quranic _āyāt_ of Imam Ibn
al-Qayyim into one volume that was first published in 1949 in Egypt. This book
was widely welcomed and lauded by peers and scholars alike, such that it became
part of the curriculum for many institutions of learning across the world. He
embarked on doing the same for the works of Shaykh al-Islām Ibn Taymīyah, but
could not complete it. Likewise, his other works on grammar (_naḥw_) and
rhetoric (_balāghah_) were also left incomplete because of his bad health.

In 1961, Shaykh Owais became a member of both the Editorial Board of the journal
Maʿārif and the Executive Committee of Dār al-Muṣannifīn. During his stay at Dār
al-Muṣannifīn, he authored numerous articles on Quranic studies, including:

- Translations of the Quran
- The Bible, the Quran, and Ḥadīth
- The Reality of the Zindīq
- Ḥāfiẓ Jalāl al-Dīn al-Suyūṭī \hon{\M_HIM}{\BODY}
- Kalimatullāh
- Imam Ibn Jarīr al-Ṭabarī \hon{\M_HIM}{\BODY}
- The Orientalist Nöldeke and the Quran
- Some Observations on Tafsīr al-Rāzī

Sheikh Owais, as a teacher, had an effect on the lives of his students not only
by virtue of his knowledge but also because of his immaculate manners and
immense care towards nurturing them. Many of his students recall how his classes
used to be an arena of enlightenment and learning that everyone looked forward
to, as it was filled with Quranic gems and beautified with poetry.

A student recalls, "A distinctive habit of Shaykh Owais was that he would always
bring his own Quran [copy] to class, unlike other teachers who would ask
students for the text. The margins of his Quran [copy] were filled with
carefully written notes and insights. He believed that a successful teacher must
prepare thoroughly, reviewing the lesson, identifying key points, and
highlighting important passages, and he practiced this consistently."

Shaykh Owais was known for always motivating, pushing, supporting, and
empowering the youth to study and learn about Islam in any manner possible.
Despite the morning classes at the institution, Shaykh Owais also taught
students at his home after ʿAṣr and Maghrib prayers. He was also known to take
special care of orphans. All of this was done so that the students could excel
and carry the Prophetic legacy forward.

Shaykh Owais passed away on the first of Ramaḍān in the year 1396\SC{h} (27
August 1976). His body was washed by the esteemed Shaykh Manẓūr Nuʿmānī
\hon{\M_HIM}{\BODY}, and he was laid to rest in Lucknow. His _janāzah_ was led
by Shaykh ʿAbd al-Ḥasan ʿAlī Nadwī \hon{\M_HIM}{\BODY} on 28 August 1976. After
the burial, Shaykh Manẓūr Nuʿmānī \hon{\M_HIM}{\BODY} said, "It was because of
the Shaykh's stronghold on adhering and propagating the creed (_aqīdah_) of
Tawḥīd that his face was so relaxed and radiant when laid to rest. It did not
appear at all that he had endured such a long illness." (Kārvān-e-Zindagī)

May Allāh have Mercy on the Shaykh and his family, and elevate his ranks in
Jannah.
